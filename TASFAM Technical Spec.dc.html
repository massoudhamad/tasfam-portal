TASFAM web portal: technical specification and CMS build brief

Next.js public site, Django admin back office, PostgreSQL, deployed on Revolutionary Government of Zanzibar infrastructure. Version 1.0 — issued for developer procurement and build.

Companion document: TASFAM build prompt (machine-executable instruction set for an AI coding agent) and the approved HTML design prototype.

1. Scope

The portal is the public face of the Tanzania Scaling-up Sustainable Fisheries and Aquaculture Management project and the disclosure channel required under the project's environmental and social commitment plan. It must serve three audiences at once: coastal communities and fishers, the project management team and implementing ministries, and financing partners and oversight bodies.

In scope: bilingual content management, news and events, a publications library, tender notices, field stories, partners, results and KPI figures, an interactive sub-project map, a grievance redress mechanism with full case workflow, public case-status lookup, staff accounts with roles and an audit trail, and email notifications.

Out of scope for phase one: public user accounts, online payment, a mobile application, and integration with the SAMAKI catch-data system beyond a read-only figures import.

2. Architecture

Layer Choice and rationale
Public frontendNext.js 15, App Router, TypeScript, React Server Components. Static generation with incremental revalidation for all public pages; server actions only for the grievance form.
Back officeDjango 5 with the built-in admin as the CMS, Django REST Framework for the read API. Chosen for a small team: permissions, audit, and forms come out of the box and are maintainable by a single developer.
DatabasePostgreSQL 16 with PostGIS for sub-project geometry. Nightly encrypted dumps, 30-day retention, quarterly restore test.
FilesLocal disk or on-premise S3-compatible object storage (MinIO). Public documents served through the web server; grievance attachments served only through an authenticated Django view, never by direct URL.
RuntimeDocker Compose on the RGoZ / e-GA server: nginx, next, django (gunicorn), postgres, redis, worker. No external cloud dependency at runtime.
Network zonesPublic site on the DMZ. Django admin bound to the government network or VPN only, on a separate hostname (admin.tasfam.go.tz), never linked from the public site.

Data flow: editors work in Django admin → publish triggers a webhook to Next.js → affected routes revalidate. If the backend is unreachable, the last generated pages continue to serve; only the grievance form degrades, and it must then show the phone and in-person channels instead of failing silently.

3. Cross-cutting requirements

  • Bilingual by construction. Every editable string exists in English and Kiswahili. Kiswahili is not an afterthought: if a translation is missing the site falls back to English and the admin list view flags the record as incomplete.
  • Low bandwidth. Budget: under 300 KB of JavaScript and under 1 MB total on first load of the home page over 3G. Images served as WebP with width variants; the map loads tiles only on interaction.
  • Accessibility. WCAG 2.1 AA. Minimum 44 px touch targets, 4.5:1 text contrast, visible focus rings, full keyboard operation, correct language attributes per locale.
  • Data residency. All personal data, and all grievance data without exception, stays on government infrastructure in Tanzania. No third-party analytics, fonts, maps, or form services that transmit visitor data abroad. Self-host fonts and map tiles.
  • Disclosure integrity. Safeguards documents are versioned; superseded versions remain retrievable with their disclosure date.

5. Grievance workflow

States: received → acknowledged → under_review → action_agreed → resolved → closed, with referred and appealed as branches and reopened from closed. Service standard: acknowledge within 7 days, substantive response within 30 days; the system computes due_at and escalates to the safeguards lead at 80% of the window and to the project coordinator on breach.

Every state change writes a CaseEvent. Where the complainant gave contact details and consented, an acknowledgement and each externally visible update is sent by email or SMS in their preferred language. Notification text never contains the complaint description, the category, or any attachment — only the reference number and a neutral status line.

5.1 GBV, SEA and SH cases

Cases in the harassment, gender-based violence, or sexual exploitation and abuse categories are handled on a survivor-centred basis and are a separate track from the moment of intake:

  • The case is flagged is_sensitive automatically and is invisible in every list, search, export, and dashboard to all users except the named GBV focal point and the project coordinator. Other safeguards staff see only that a sensitive case exists and its reference.
  • The portal does not investigate or record incident detail beyond what the complainant volunteers. The public form shows, for this category, the referral pathway to the designated service provider before the description field, and states plainly that the project will not contact anyone without the complainant's consent.
  • No email or SMS notification is generated for sensitive cases unless the complainant explicitly opted in; internal notification names no detail and is sent to one recipient only.
  • Every view of a sensitive case is written to the audit log, including read access. Exports of sensitive cases are disabled in the admin.
  • Retention: personal identifiers on sensitive cases are purged on a defined schedule after closure, leaving anonymised statistics only. The schedule is set by the safeguards lead and enforced by a scheduled job.

The developer implements the mechanism; the handling protocol, referral pathway, and retention period are supplied by the project's safeguards lead and must be confirmed in writing before go-live.

6. Roles and permissions

Role Can do

Editor / publisherEverything above plus publish and unpublish, manage menus and featured content.
Procurement officerFull control of tenders and award records only.
M&E officerIndicators, indicator values, evidence upload, sub-project progress; can mark values verified.
Safeguards officerNon-sensitive grievance cases: triage, assign, record events, respond, close. Publish safeguards documents.
GBV focal pointSole handler of sensitive cases. No content permissions.
Project coordinatorRead everything including sensitive cases, approve escalations, view audit log and SLA reports.
AdministratorUser and role management, system settings. No default access to case content; granting it to oneself is itself audited.

All staff accounts require two-factor authentication. Sessions expire after 30 minutes idle. Five failed logins lock the account for 15 minutes and notify the administrator. No shared accounts.

7. Public API

Read endpoints are cached, unauthenticated, and return published records only. All accept ?lang=en|sw. Write endpoints are limited to grievance intake.

Endpoint Purpose
GET /api/v1/news, /news/{slug}Paginated list and detail, filterable by category and year
GET /api/v1/publicationsLibrary with type, year, safeguards filters
GET /api/v1/tendersOpen and closed notices, awards
GET /api/v1/components, /sub-projectsComponent detail; sub-projects also served as GeoJSON at /sub-projects.geojson
GET /api/v1/indicatorsPublic indicators with verified values, baseline, target, time series
GET /api/v1/stories, /partners, /pages/{slug}Stories, partners, editable static pages
POST /api/v1/grievancesIntake. Rate limited to 5 per hour per IP, CAPTCHA-free but honeypot and timing protected, returns only the reference number
GET /api/v1/grievances/statusLookup by reference plus a second factor (the phone number given, or a passphrase set at submission). Returns status and public events only, never internal notes. Rate limited to 10 attempts per hour; sensitive cases return status only.
POST /api/v1/revalidateSigned webhook from Django on publish; triggers Next.js route revalidation

Anonymous submissions still receive a reference number and can be tracked with a passphrase chosen at submission. If the complainant loses both, the case cannot be retrieved — this is stated on the form.

8. Notifications

  • Transactional email via the government SMTP relay; SMS via a locally contracted gateway. Both queued through Redis with retry, so a failed send never blocks a submission.
  • Templates are bilingual and content-managed. Recipient language follows the case's preferred language.
  • Triggers: grievance acknowledged, status changed to a visible state, response issued, SLA at 80%, SLA breached, new tender published (subscribers), weekly digest to the project coordinator.
  • No grievance content in any message body. No attachments by email.

9. Interactive map

MapLibre GL with self-hosted vector tiles, or a static basemap where tile hosting is not available. Sub-projects are drawn from PostGIS as GeoJSON, clustered by district, filterable by component, island, and status. Each marker opens a card with name, component, status, progress and budget. The map must degrade to a searchable list view when WebGL or the tile server is unavailable, and must never call an external tile provider.

10. Security

  • HTTPS only, HSTS, secure and same-site cookies, a strict content security policy with no inline scripts.
  • OWASP Top 10 addressed explicitly in the developer's test plan; dependency scanning in CI; an independent penetration test before go-live and after any major release.
  • Uploaded files virus-scanned (ClamAV), type-whitelisted, stored outside the web root, and served with Content-Disposition: attachment.
  • Encrypted nightly backups held off the application server; documented restore procedure with a quarterly drill.
  • Audit log is append-only and retained for the life of the project plus five years.
  • A written data protection note published on the site explains what the GRM collects, who sees it, and how long it is kept.

11. Acceptance criteria

  1. Every public page renders correctly in English and Kiswahili, with no untranslated interface strings.
  2. The communications officer can publish a news item, a tender, a publication, and a story without developer help, and the public page reflects it within one minute.
  3. A grievance submitted anonymously produces a reference number, appears in the safeguards queue, and is retrievable by the complainant through the status lookup.
  4. A case in a GBV category is invisible to the safeguards officer account and visible to the GBV focal point, and both facts are demonstrated in testing.
  5. SLA escalation fires correctly against a clock-shifted test case.
  6. Home page scores at least 90 on Lighthouse performance and 100 on accessibility, tested on a throttled 3G profile.
  7. Keyboard-only and screen-reader passes on the navigation, grievance form, map fallback, and document library.
  8. Backup restore executed successfully into a clean environment in front of the project team.
  9. Penetration test report delivered with all high and critical findings closed.
  10. Handover: source code in the government repository, deployment runbook, admin user guide in English and Kiswahili, and two half-day training sessions for the PMT.
